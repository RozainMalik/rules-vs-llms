# Income-tax provisions for resident salaried individuals — FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26)

Condensed transcription of the Income-tax Act, 1961 as amended by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024.
Each paragraph keeps its section reference so that answers can cite it.
Scope: income under the head "Salaries" and loss from one self-occupied house property only.

## Sec 17(1) — Salary
Salary includes wages, allowances (including house rent allowance), bonus and any other
payment from the employer. Gross salary is the total of all such amounts for the year.

## Sec 10(13A) read with Rule 2A — House rent allowance (old regime only)
The exempt part of HRA is the LEAST of:
(a) the HRA actually received;
(b) rent actually paid in excess of 10% of salary;
(c) 50% of salary where the rented house is in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata or Chennai, and 40% of salary elsewhere.
"Salary" for this rule means basic salary plus dearness allowance (where DA forms part of
retirement benefits). If no rent is paid, or rent does not exceed 10% of salary, nothing is exempt.

## Sec 16(ia) — Standard deduction
A deduction of fifty thousand rupees, or the amount of salary, whichever is less.
Proviso (Sec 115BAC): for an individual taxed under the new regime the amount is
seventy-five thousand rupees, or the amount of salary, whichever is less.

## Sec 24(b) and Sec 71(3A) — Interest on loan for a self-occupied house (old regime only)
Interest payable on capital borrowed to acquire or construct a self-occupied house is
deductible up to two lakh rupees. The resulting loss under "Income from house property" may be
set off against salary income only up to two lakh rupees; any balance is carried forward and
is not relevant to this year's tax.

## Gross total income
Income from salary less the house property loss set off. It cannot be negative.

## Sec 80A(2) — Overall cap on Chapter VI-A deductions
The total of deductions under Chapter VI-A cannot exceed the gross total income.

## Sec 80C read with Sec 80CCE — Investments (old regime only)
Life insurance premium, provident fund, PPF, ELSS, principal repaid on a housing loan, tuition
fees, etc. Aggregate deduction limited to one lakh fifty thousand rupees.

## Sec 80CCD(1B) — Additional NPS contribution (old regime only)
Own contribution to the National Pension System, up to fifty thousand rupees, in addition to
the Sec 80C limit.

## Sec 80D — Health insurance premium (old regime only)
(i) For self, spouse and dependent children: up to twenty-five thousand rupees; fifty thousand
rupees if the individual (or spouse) is a senior citizen (60 years or more).
(ii) For parents: a further deduction up to twenty-five thousand rupees; fifty thousand rupees
if either parent is a senior citizen.

## Sec 288A — Rounding of total income
Total income is rounded off to the nearest multiple of ten rupees: paise are ignored, then if the
last digit is five or more it is increased to the next multiple of ten, otherwise reduced.

## First Schedule, Part III, Para A — Old regime rates
(I) Individual below 60 years:
    up to 2,50,000: nil; 2,50,001–5,00,000: 5%; 5,00,001–10,00,000: 20%; above 10,00,000: 30%.
(II) Resident individual aged 60 or more but below 80 (senior citizen):
    up to 3,00,000: nil; 3,00,001–5,00,000: 5%; 5,00,001–10,00,000: 20%; above 10,00,000: 30%.
(III) Resident individual aged 80 or more (super senior citizen):
    up to 5,00,000: nil; 5,00,001–10,00,000: 20%; above 10,00,000: 30%.

## Sec 115BAC — New tax regime (default)
From FY 2023-24 the new regime is the default; an individual with salary income may opt out
each year when filing the return. Under the new regime the following are NOT allowed:
HRA exemption under Sec 10(13A), Sec 80C, Sec 80CCD(1B), Sec 80D, and interest on a
self-occupied house under Sec 24(b). The standard deduction is allowed (seventy-five thousand rupees).
Sec 115BAC(1A)(ii) rates, the same for all ages:
    up to 3,00,000: nil; 3,00,001–7,00,000: 5%; 7,00,001–10,00,000: 10%;
    10,00,001–12,00,000: 15%; 12,00,001–15,00,000: 20%; above 15,00,000: 30%.

## Sec 87A — Rebate for resident individuals
Old regime: if total income does not exceed five lakh rupees, a rebate equal to the income-tax
or twelve thousand five hundred rupees, whichever is less.
New regime: if total income does not exceed seven lakh rupees, a rebate equal to the income-tax
or twenty-five thousand rupees, whichever is less.
Marginal relief (new regime only): where total income exceeds seven lakh rupees and the
income-tax payable exceeds the amount by which total income exceeds seven lakh rupees, a rebate
equal to that excess of tax is allowed, so that tax does not exceed (total income − 7,00,000).
The rebate is applied to income-tax before surcharge and cess.

## First Schedule — Surcharge on income-tax
Old regime: 10% if total income exceeds 50 lakh; 15% if it exceeds 1 crore; 25% if it exceeds
2 crore; 37% if it exceeds 5 crore.
New regime (Sec 115BAC): same, except the rate is capped at 25% (no 37% band).
Marginal relief: where total income exceeds a threshold, the income-tax plus surcharge shall not
exceed the income-tax plus surcharge payable on a total income equal to that threshold, by more
than the amount of income that exceeds the threshold.

## Health and Education Cess
4% of the income-tax plus surcharge (after rebate and marginal relief).

## Sec 288B — Rounding of tax
The final tax payable is rounded off to the nearest multiple of ten rupees, in the same manner as Sec 288A.
